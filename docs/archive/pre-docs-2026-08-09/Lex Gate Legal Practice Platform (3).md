# 1. Purpose

This document defines the process for discovering, recording, evaluating, and validating how Lex Gate currently operates.

Its purpose is to establish a reliable current-state baseline before the project defines the future product, detailed workflows, architecture, or Software Requirements Specification.

The discovery process must answer:

- what Lex Gate does;
- who performs each activity;
- how clients reach and engage the firm;
- how conflicts are checked;
- how matters and cases are created;
- how deadlines and hearings are monitored;
- where documents and evidence are stored;
- how communications are conducted;
- how fees, invoices, payments, and expenses are managed;
- how access to confidential information is controlled;
- how employees and lawyers are administered;
- which processes are formal and which depend on memory or habit;
- which tools currently hold client or matter data;
- where operational, legal, privacy, and security risks exist;
- which existing practices should be preserved, redesigned, or discontinued.

This document is not intended to force Lex Gate’s work into a generic law-firm template.

It is intended to accurately describe the firm’s real operating model, including exceptions, informal workarounds, unusual matter types, and differences between lawyers.

# 4. Discovery Principles

## 4.1 Observe Before Redesigning

Interview answers describe how people believe work happens.

Evidence and observation show how it actually happens.

Both must be collected.

---

## 4.2 Capture Exceptions

A process map that describes only the ideal path is insufficient.

Discovery must identify:

- urgent matters;
- incomplete client information;
- clients without email;
- clients using only WhatsApp or telephone;
- matters involving multiple clients;
- clients with conflicting interests;
- minors;
- incarcerated clients;
- foreign clients;
- companies with multiple representatives;
- documents arriving after deadlines;
- postponed hearings;
- payment disputes;
- clients who stop responding;
- lawyer absence;
- employee departure;
- system or internet failure.

---

## 4.3 Avoid Blame

The objective is to understand the system, not evaluate individuals.

Discovery records should describe:

- the process;
- the control;
- the evidence;
- the risk;

rather than accusing a person of failure.

---

## 4.4 Preserve Confidentiality

Discovery itself may expose sensitive client and matter information.

The discovery team shall:

- request sanitized samples where possible;
- avoid copying entire client files unnecessarily;
- use matter codes instead of names;
- restrict interview notes;
- encrypt collected evidence;
- prohibit uploading evidence to unapproved AI or cloud tools;
- delete temporary copies after controlled incorporation;
- record who accessed discovery material.

---

## 4.5 Separate Fact, Interpretation, and Proposal

Every discovery statement shall be classified as one of:

- **Observed fact**
- **Participant statement**
- **Document-supported fact**
- **Analyst interpretation**
- **Unverified assumption**
- **Future proposal**

These classifications must not be silently mixed.

# 26. Interview Protocol

## 26.1 Interview Rules

- Explain that the goal is process understanding.
- Do not ask participants to disclose unnecessary client names.
- Request examples.
- Ask what happens when the normal process fails.
- Ask participants to show the actual tool or document.

# 29. Current Known Risks and Hypotheses

The following are hypotheses only and must be investigated.

## HYP-001 — Fragmented Communications

Client communications may be spread among Facebook, WhatsApp, telephone, email, and personal devices.

**Potential consequence:** incomplete matter history and confidentiality exposure.

---

## HYP-002 — Informal Deadline Tracking

Hearings and deadlines may depend on personal calendars, paper diaries, or memory.

**Potential consequence:** missed or poorly escalated deadlines.

---

## HYP-003 — Unstructured Document Storage

Matter files may be spread among paper, devices, messaging applications, and cloud folders.

**Potential consequence:** duplicate files, uncertain versions, slow retrieval, and weak custody evidence.

---

## HYP-004 — Broad Access

Access may be based on possession of a folder, device, or shared account rather than assigned matter responsibility.

**Potential consequence:** unnecessary disclosure.

---

## HYP-005 — Incomplete Conflict Evidence

Conflict checks may occur informally without preserving search terms, reviewers, or decisions.

**Potential consequence:** professional risk and inability to prove appropriate screening.

---

## HYP-006 — Inconsistent Financial Records

Fee agreements, invoices, receipts, expenses, and payment records may be maintained in separate systems.

**Potential consequence:** reconciliation errors and tax compliance difficulty.

---

## HYP-007 — Brand Inconsistency

Public materials may not yet use a fully controlled bilingual identity.

**Potential consequence:** reduced trust, inconsistent legal claims, and difficult website migration.

# Appendix C — Risk Rating

## Critical

A current condition could plausibly cause:

- loss of confidentiality;
- missed legal deadline;
- unauthorized legal act;
- major data breach;
- irrecoverable record loss;
- serious tax or regulatory exposure.

## High

A condition is likely to cause material client, legal, financial, or operational harm.

## Medium

A condition causes inefficiency, inconsistency, or manageable risk.

## Low

A limited inconvenience or improvement opportunity with no immediate material harm.

# Appendix D — Discovery Rule

The future platform shall not be designed around a statement merely because:

> “This is how we usually do it.”

The statement must be evaluated to determine whether the practice is:

- legally required;
- professionally required;
- operationally valuable;
- historically accidental;
- a workaround;
- inefficient;
- unsafe;
- obsolete.
